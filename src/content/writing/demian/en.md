---
title: A Deep Dive into Myself
---

I finally read *Demian*, a book I had been saving for later.

I was glad I had waited until I could read it as the person I am now. It left an impression that made the wait worthwhile.

Even those who have never read *Demian* have probably come across these words:

*“The bird fights its way out of the egg. The egg is the world. Whoever will be born must destroy a world.”*

Before reading the book, I had taken this passage to mean that its message was a plausible enough lesson about breaking free of rigid thinking and convention, and moving toward something new.

But the further I read, the more I felt that what *Demian*—what Hermann Hesse—was really trying to say was not about breaking out of something, but about going inward, deeper and deeper. Throughout the novel, words reminding me that what we must explore to the very end is ourselves reached me through Demian's remarks, Sinclair's inner monologues, and sometimes Pistorius's cries.

It also made me wonder whether this is what makes a novel so special: an author's thoughts, discoveries, and insights find their way through different characters, taking the form of sentences that each have a charm of their own.

For nearly a month, I had been preoccupied with my future, my dreams, and the direction of my career. I had a strong sense that this was one of those rare moments, one of those opportunities, that could profoundly alter the course of my life. It was also a time when I needed to think about what I wanted my life to be for. Before deciding what dreams and what kind of work I wanted to pursue, I wanted to understand myself as fully as I could. I wanted to know what made me happy, what I valued, and what made my heart race.

I keep a notebook in which I still make sure to write at least a page every day. Its pages hold insights, resolutions, and plans drawn from everyday moments: fragments of who I am. (I plan to write about these notebooks another time.) Starting with the earliest of them, I went through the pages one by one, unearthing sentences that helped explain who I was. I gathered them together and kept returning to them, taking my time to dig deeper. Some broke down into more fundamental layers. Others became more abstract and more general. Still others came together to form a fuller meaning.

It was work that took a very long time. So I was happy to feel that, through a single book, Hesse was telling me it was well worth doing. It helped me trust myself.

Those notebooks also hold passages I have treasured from the books I read. Here are a few from *Demian* that resonated deeply with me.

> “I am only interested in the steps I took in life, in order to attain self-realization. All the pretty resting-places, happy isles and children’s paradises, whose charm is not unknown to me, I leave lying behind me in the shimmer of a distant horizon, and I have no desire to set foot there again.” — p. 65 in the Korean edition I read

> “Wise speeches have no value at all, absolutely none. […] To escape from yourself is a sin. You should be able to creep right into yourself, like a tortoise.” — p. 88 in the Korean edition I read

> “However, it was not this learned instruction which was of service to my inner self, but rather the contrary. What did me good was the self-progression I made, the increasing confidence in my own dreams, thoughts and presentiments, and the consciousness of the power that I carried in me.” — p. 161 in the Korean edition I read

*The English quotations are from the [1923 edition of Demian](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/74222/74222-h/74222-h.htm), available through Project Gutenberg. The page numbers refer to the Korean edition I read; the ellipsis marks an omission from the English text.*
