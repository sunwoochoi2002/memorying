---
title: Facing.
---

There are so many things I want to do.

And I find my greatest joy in trying this and that, seeing sides of the world I never knew before, and growing along the way. (Is there a job whose whole work is having new experiences?)

But when you find yourself in so many situations and meet so many people, there are times when certain people make you feel something beyond the simple pleasure of meeting someone new: happiness, wonder, envy.

Then, out of a bold wish to become like them, people who are living objectively wonderful lives, I catch myself at some point measuring myself by their standards.

Then a thought struck me: I need to define this abstract, conceptual self of mine well, and make my identity and the values I pursue clear. So that when I see someone else's light, I am not cast into shadow but can shine alongside them.

So here I am on the KTX down to Pohang after wrapping up GSSC, laptop open, just writing whatever comes.

In a way, this is written on a sudden impulse, so my thinking may change again later, but for now I think I can define my identity as "a person who faces things."

In particular, a person who faces people, situations, and pain.

## First, people.

I meet many people face to face and hear about their lives from them. Everyone has their own story, and everyone is the protagonist of their own world.

Yet many people still do not know they are the protagonist, or have never thought to turn the cover of the book called "me."

This, too, has to do with "facing."

And hearing stories I have never lived through is just as enjoyable as experiencing things myself.

## Second, situations.

Since the third element is "pain," here I want to talk about situations that are positive or neutral.

My threshold for happiness is fairly low. Just walking under a blue sky full of fluffy clouds, soaking up the warm sunlight, makes me happy, and even now, as I write this while listening to a song I love, I feel happy.

I try to see the situations I find myself in at every moment, the ones that "could be neutral," in a positive light, and to embrace them.

## Lastly, pain.

Humans have long been creatures that had to avoid danger.

Early humans who stood and faced a charging mammoth did not survive, and I believe that trait has naturally been imprinted on us modern people as well.

But modern society is different. Not only is a mammoth never going to come charging at me, but in everyday life our lives are threatened far less often than in the distant past.

But our nature has stayed the same. When we see something hard, tedious, or unpleasant, we naturally want to just avoid it. In all sorts of situations.

But if we avoid it, nothing changes. Our thinking does not broaden, and we do not grow.

Society is no longer what it was long ago, when survival was humanity's one and only reason. So survival is not the goal of life, and I believe that for me to keep growing, my instinct to run when I face pain has to change as well.

Whenever I feel pain of any kind (especially emotional pain), I face it and look at it fully. With an attitude like, "Let's see what kind of feeling you are, to make things this hard for me."

Surprisingly, once I face pain and look it straight in the eye, it no longer hurts the way it did before. Then, within the "given situation" of pain, I think about what I can do next and put it into action. Only after this process became second nature did I finally understand what "No pain, no gain" means.

On impulse, I have put into words how I see my identity right now. It may be messy and rambling, but I feel once again how important it is to leave my thoughts in writing, even like this.

I want to thank the person who, after a long while, gave me the motivation to write again.

The photo shows a song I have recently fallen for, and the one I kept playing on repeat while writing this.
