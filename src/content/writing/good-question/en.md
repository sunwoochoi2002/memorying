---
title: Good Question
---

These days I find myself talking with a lot more people.

Being in the startup scene, I join the occasional ideathon, and now and then I go out to meetups. Along the way, I have been thinking a lot lately about soft skills, which are often described as communication skills.

When you talk with someone, you notice far more than you might expect. Their eyes, where they look, how their hands move, how upright they hold themselves, and so on.

Put those together well, and you can tell whether the person is truly engaged with what you are saying or just responding out of politeness. No, it is more accurate to say you can simply feel it.

They may just be trying to be polite, but apart from that, I know how boring it can sometimes be to talk with someone who is not really engaged, so I always try to be someone people can genuinely engage with.

And I think that is possible, to some extent, through effort: through "good questions."

To borrow a phrase, I think humans are creatures who are "dying to talk." After all, even the king from that famous storybook could not hold it in and ended up shouting into the bamboo forest.

So simply by listening well and asking good questions instead of talking about ourselves, we can avoid becoming one of those creatures "dying to talk." And naturally, the people we talk with are more likely to feel engaged in the conversation.

Maybe that is why, these days, hearing "That's a good question!" or "What a good question!" feels so nice and makes me proud. And I try to say "That's a good question" often myself.

In the U.S., saying "Good question!" comes very naturally, but I do not think it does in Korea yet. I want to help change that, little by little.
