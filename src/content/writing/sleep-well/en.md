title: Why We Sleep
---

A question suddenly came to me. I suspect most people are the same, but even when the situation is exactly the same, my feelings and judgments seem to differ between when I am short on sleep and tired, and when I have slept well, have even exercised, and feel a little uplifted.

So I asked ChatGPT the following:

> Q. Between the negative feelings and skeptical judgment of my situation that come when I am short on sleep and tired, and the positive feelings and optimistic judgment of my situation that come when I have slept enough, exercised, and feel slightly uplifted, which is closer to my original (true) feelings/situation?

It gave a long answer grounded in neuroscience, but contrary to what I had in mind, its conclusion was not an either-or.

The feelings I have when I am sleep-deprived and the feelings I have when I am slightly uplifted after exercise are both truly my own. According to research, sleep deprivation consistently lowers positive emotions and increases things like anxiety. Right after exercise, on the other hand, positive emotions really do rise. What struck me, though, was that the two are not symmetrical distortions. Sleep deprivation is a state in which normal brain function has not yet fully recovered, whereas the good mood after moderate exercise is closer to a normal emotional change that arises from a healthy physiological state. Of course, after exercising, one may lean a little further toward optimism than usual.

So the best reference point for judgment, it said, is **a state in which you have slept enough and feel physically well, but are neither especially elated nor low**.

When we wrestle with a worry, sometimes it starts to seem like no big deal and courage to overcome the situation wells up, while at other times we end up taking the same situation more seriously without even realizing it.

But now we know: if what we are agonizing over feels too painful and hard, it may be a sign of tiredness sent by our body and brain. So, especially with long-term problems, it is probably wise to think them over in a calm state after a good night’s sleep, and to trust the thoughts that keep returning when we are in a similar condition.

I close by strongly recommending ***Why We Sleep***, which yc (yoonchulyi.com) recommends as the best book of a lifetime.
