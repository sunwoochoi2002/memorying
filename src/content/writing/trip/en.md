---
title: Journey
---

Just now, right before bed, a Short I stopped by for a moment moved me in a way I did not expect, so I opened my laptop to record this moment.

It was a comment on a Short about *The Intern*, a film I watched long ago and found deeply memorable. The comment was about "journeys."

> Most people do not know what a true journey is, so they leave their daily lives behind on vacation. When they come back, they console themselves by saying, "That was great!" It takes many years to realize that the true journey is the everyday life that unfolds each day.
>
> If the reason you wake up this morning is to go to work, that is where the journey begins. Nothing could be better than this. And if, on your day off, you play golf with your wife, and on the next day off you watch a good movie and go out to eat together, you are surely enjoying the finest journey there is.
>
> <small>— A comment I happened to come across on YouTube</small>

Ben, the film's protagonist, had been the head of a printing company and had retired in his old age. After losing his wife, he struggled with deep loneliness and spent three years traveling the world, trying to fill the emptiness. But no one was waiting to welcome him home, and he only came face to face with an even greater loneliness. Then he happened to see a posting for senior interns, applied, and became an intern at a startup. *The Intern* is a film about the many everyday episodes of this 70-year-old senior intern.

I imagine the comment above starts from the trip Ben set out on to fill that emptiness. And Ben, too, probably came to feel what the comment says deep in his bones only after he returned from his travels. (He is only a character in a film, of course.)

I am not in my seventies, and I still have much of my youth ahead of me, yet the comment resonated with me so deeply.

Why was that?

I tried to think it over carefully. No, actually, I already knew the reason without having to think hard about it.

Every single day that I breathe and live is so precious to me. There may be many ways to explain why life is precious, but I find my reason in the people around me.

Nothing lasts forever, and neither do people. From the good friends who lean on and trust each other, all the way to my own family. Our circumstances and surroundings may change until we can no longer see each other, and there are so many other reasons we may have to say goodbye to someone.

That is why every day I spend with these people is so precious to me.

Because I know these relationships can (quite literally) end someday, and that this is time with an end.

An Instagram post on a similar theme just came to mind. I think it was an Instagram comic about a partner who never got angry. (It happened to show up in my feed.)

Someone asked their partner, who rarely got angry, "Why do you hardly ever get angry?"

The answer really stayed with me.

I do not remember the exact words, but

> "Because I know our time has an end, and this moment is just so precious."

I remember the answer being something like that.

I feel the same way. These moments with the people I love are so precious to me. Maybe that is why I rarely get angry.

This is a bit scattered because I am writing things down as they come to me, but I really wanted to get it down.

I thought I was someone who felt the happiness of each day fairly fully. But lately, just because life has gotten a little busier, I caught myself living only for the weekend and not fully enjoying the present.

*'When did I become such a typical office worker...'* I thought.

Let me go back to who I was in my first week as an intern.

And beyond that, let me treat each day as more precious and spend every day loving my people.

There are so many times when things are hard and nothing goes as planned. But then again, maybe that is just life's default.

I will close by sharing something interesting that Conor Neill said in one of his talks.

> If you feel inside yourself a surge in emotion,
> I want you to practice using the word
>
> interesting.
>
> <small>— Conor Neill</small>
